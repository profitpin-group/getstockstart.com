Keeping material costs current when supplier prices go up

How do I update material costs in my pricebook when my supplier raises prices?

Start from the newest supplier invoice, not from memory or an old price sheet: the invoice is what you actually paid. For each line, find the matching item in your pricebook by part number, check that the invoice unit matches the pricebook unit, and change the cost only on items where it really moved. In ServiceTitan most shops do this through the pricebook export and import rather than editing items one at a time. The weekly invoice routine walks through it step by step.

How often should a contractor update pricebook material costs?

There is no single rule. Published guidance commonly suggests reviewing material pricing at least quarterly, and more often for copper, wire and other materials whose prices move quickly. A practical rhythm is to match the update to your deliveries: if invoices arrive every week, a weekly pass keeps each update small, while a month of invoices left to pile up becomes an afternoon of work.

Why don't my pricebook costs match my supplier invoices?

Usually one of four things. The supplier changed the price and nobody updated the item; the invoice is priced per box, case or roll while the pricebook counts each, foot or pound; the same part exists twice in the pricebook and the wrong copy was updated; or the cost was changed in one place while a different vendor cost is the one being used. Check the unit first, because a pack-size mismatch looks exactly like a huge price change.

Does ServiceTitan update material costs automatically from supplier invoices?

ServiceTitan offers supplier catalog integrations for some suppliers, and its help center describes cost sync and price-change options for those. If your supplier is not connected, or you buy from several supply houses, costs generally change only when someone updates them, by hand or through an import file. Check ServiceTitan's own documentation for what your account and suppliers support.

What if my supply house isn't integrated with ServiceTitan?

Then the invoices are your price source. Shops typically export the pricebook, update costs in that workbook from the week's invoices, and import it back; ServiceTitan's help center also documents a vendor-material cost import for suppliers whose price changes arrive as a spreadsheet. StockStart is built for this case: it reads invoices from any supplier against your exported pricebook and prepares the changes for you to review and import yourself.

The best template is your own pricebook export. It already has the exact column names, item IDs, codes and categories ServiceTitan accepts back, so patching changes into it avoids the failed or silent imports a blank sheet causes. The import checklist lists what to check before the file goes in, and there is a printable weekly checklist PDF.

Box vs each, unit costs and price changes that look wrong

Why is the cost per unit wrong after I updated the pricebook?

The most common cause is a pack price entered as the cost of one item: a box of 25 fittings at $84.50 typed into an item that is sold each. Every quote using that part is then off by a factor of 25 until someone notices. Converting the invoice price to the pricebook's unit before writing the cost prevents it.

How do I convert a box, case or roll price to a cost per each?

Divide the invoice price by the number of units in the pack, counted the same way the pricebook counts the item. A box of 100 elbows at $84.50 is $0.845 each; a 100 ft roll at $62.00 is $0.62 per foot if the pricebook item is per foot, but stays $62.00 if the item is the whole roll. The pack quantity is often printed in the description or the unit-of-measure column on the invoice, so read it rather than guessing.

How do I tell a real price increase from a data entry mistake?

Compare the size of the change with the pack quantity. If the new cost is roughly 10, 12, 25 or 100 times the old one, it is almost always a unit problem, not a price increase. A real supplier increase is usually a modest percentage and often shows across several items from the same supplier at once. When in doubt, check the line on the original invoice before changing the pricebook.

What percentage price change should I flag for review?

Pick a threshold that fits your shop and your materials; there is no industry standard. A low threshold catches more but gives you more to look at, while a high one can let a real increase on an expensive item slip through. The free checker lets you set your own percent, and StockStart flags cost changes above the threshold you choose.

Duplicate items, matching part numbers and pricebook imports

How do I stop duplicate parts showing up in my pricebook?

Duplicates appear when a part is added as new because a search didn't find it under a different description, such as "3/4 CU 90" and "Copper elbow 3/4". Match every invoice line against the pricebook by part number and unit before adding anything, treat a description-only match as a suggestion for a person to confirm, and check the batch against itself so an invoice received by email and through a portal isn't entered twice. More on this in matching supplier invoice lines to pricebook items.

How do I match supplier part numbers to my pricebook items?

Search the vendor part number first, then the manufacturer part number, then a normalized description. A part number match with a matching unit is a safe link; anything weaker should wait for a person. Keeping vendor and manufacturer part numbers in their own fields, exactly as printed, makes every later match easier.

Why did my pricebook import fail, or change nothing?

Common causes are a file built from a blank sheet instead of the export, renamed or reordered column headers, missing item IDs so updates create new items instead of changing existing ones, and items pointing at a category that doesn't exist. ServiceTitan's help center also has an article on costs that don't populate after an import, since vendor costs can be stored per vendor. Import a small batch first and open a few items to check unit, cost, category and active flag.

Can I turn supplier invoice PDFs or photos into a pricebook update?

Yes, but read the values before trusting them: a creased photo or an unusual invoice layout can be misread by any OCR. StockStart reads PDFs, photos, emailed receipts and spreadsheets, keeps the source image next to each line, and holds uncertain values in a review queue instead of guessing; it does not claim 100% OCR accuracy. The guide to supplier invoice PDFs to pricebook updates covers the process.

How do I bulk update material prices in ServiceTitan?

The usual way to change many costs at once is the export and import: export materials, change costs in that workbook, keep the IDs and headers as they are, and import it back. Some shops also adjust markups in bulk after costs change. Whichever way you do it, keep the export you started from and the file you imported, so you can see afterwards what changed.

Margins, time spent and a record of what changed

What happens to my margins if material costs in the pricebook are out of date?

Prices built on old costs quietly lose margin on every job that uses the item. For example, a part with a $10.00 cost and a $20.00 price earns a 50% margin; if the real cost becomes $11.00 and the pricebook isn't updated, the margin is 45% without anyone deciding that. Markup is calculated on cost and margin on the selling price, so check both after a round of increases.

How long does a manual pricebook cost update take?

It scales with invoice lines, not invoices. Twenty lines is a short break; two hundred lines from several suppliers can take an afternoon each week, and it is the searching for each part that takes the time, not the typing. The full routine and where it breaks are in updating a ServiceTitan pricebook by hand.

How can I see when a cost changed and which invoice it came from?

Only if you keep a record. Save the pricebook export you started from and the update file you imported for each run; together they answer "when did this cost change, and why". StockStart keeps both files for each run and traces every row to its source invoice.

Are the quantities on supplier invoices the same as stock on hand?

Not necessarily. An invoice records a purchase; receipts, credits, returns and what has already gone out on trucks all change the picture. Invoice history is good for keeping costs current, but it cannot by itself tell you current inventory.
